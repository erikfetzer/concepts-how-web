Sixty-two powers of ten, and we’re in the middle.

From the smallest length physics can talk about to the edge of what we can see spans about 10⁶² times. Humans sit near the middle of that range. Zoom through it, squeeze 13.8 billion years into a calendar, and meet the objects that break intuition.

Demo 2 · time

The cosmic calendar

Squeeze the universe’s 13.8 billion years into one calendar year, as Carl Sagan did in The Dragons of Eden (1977). The Big Bang is midnight on 1 January. Today is the last stroke of midnight on 31 December. Each day is about 38 million years; each second, about 440 years.

Demo 3 · gravity

How small would you have to squeeze it?

Anything becomes a black hole if it’s compressed inside its Schwarzschild radius, r = 2GM/c². That radius grows in direct proportion to mass, about 3 km for every Sun’s worth.

Demo 4 · cosmology

Space is expanding, and there’s no centre

In 1929 Edwin Hubble found that distant galaxies recede faster the farther away they are. It isn’t an explosion from one spot: space itself stretches, so every galaxy sees all the others moving away. Tap any galaxy to stand on it.

Hubble’s lawSpeed = H₀ × distance. Every arrow is proportional to its galaxy’s distance from you, wherever “you” are. Today H₀ is about 70 km/s for every megaparsec (3.26 million light-years).
The Hubble tensionMeasurements from the early universe’s afterglow give about 67 km/s/Mpc; measurements of nearby supernovae give about 73. The gap has survived years of checking, and may point to new physics.
